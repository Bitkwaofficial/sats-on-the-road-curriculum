# Facilitator Guide, Session 1: Money and why ours fails

**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Audience:** Day 1, everyone (market traders, students, workers, ages about 15 to 55). No prior knowledge needed.
**Objectives:**
1. Learners can say in their own words what money is and the three jobs it does (store of value, medium of exchange, unit of account).
2. Learners can name the properties that make money good or bad (durable, divisible, portable, acceptable, scarce, fungible).
3. Learners can explain how money moved from barter to commodity money to fiat.
4. Learners can describe, using the local currency, how printing more money lowers what their money can buy.

**Materials:**
- A few shells, coins, or small objects to pass around.
- Slips of paper as play money for the auction, and one small real prize (a sweet, a soda, a fruit).
- Flip chart or chalkboard, markers or chalk.
- Printed handouts, one per learner.
- Slides (optional, `slides.md`) if a screen is available.

**Diploma source:** BitKwa Bitcoin Diploma, Chapter 1 (Money: history, what it is, why we need it) and Chapter 2 (Fiat money, its problems, and the search for solutions).

## Minute by minute

### 0 to 5, Welcome and one question (5 min)
Welcome the room warmly. Ask one question and take a few answers out loud:
"What is the most useful thing you own that is not money?"
Then ask, "Could you pay for your lunch with it today?" This opens the idea that money is the thing everyone will accept.

Talking points:
- We use money every day but rarely stop to ask what it really is.
- Today we look at what money is, why it was invented, and why the money most of us use is losing value.

### 5 to 13, What money is and the three jobs it does (8 min)
Explain money as a shared agreement, a tool for trade, not a thing with value in itself.

Talking points:
- Money is a way to exchange goods and services and to measure their value.
- It works because everyone agrees to accept it.
- The three jobs of money:
  - **Store of value:** it should hold its worth so you can save for later.
  - **Medium of exchange:** you can trade it for anything, so you do not need to find someone who wants exactly what you have.
  - **Unit of account:** it gives one shared way to price things and compare them.

### 13 to 20, Barter to commodity to fiat (7 min)
Tell the story simply, in three steps.

Talking points:
- **Barter:** long ago people swapped goods directly. This only works if each person wants what the other has, the "double coincidence of wants." It is slow and clumsy.
- **Commodity money:** people picked one good thing everyone accepted, like cowry shells, gold, or silver. Cowry shells were used across parts of Africa for a very long time. These are scarce and hard to fake.
- **Fiat money:** today our notes and coins are "fiat," which means "by decree." They are money because the government says so, not because they are backed by gold. The link between paper money and gold ended over the last century.
- Draw out the properties of good money on the board: durable, divisible, portable, acceptable, scarce, fungible. Pass the objects around and ask which properties each one has.

### 20 to 30, The inflation auction (10 min)
Run the activity in `exercise.md`. In short: give every learner the same amount of play money, auction one small real prize, note the winning price. Then hand everyone a lot more play money and auction an identical prize again. The price jumps.

Facilitator notes:
- Keep it lively and quick.
- Write both winning prices on the board, round one and round two.
- Do not explain the lesson yet. Let them feel the price rise first.

### 30 to 38, Debrief with the local currency (8 min)
Connect the auction to real life, using `[local currency]` as the example.

Talking points:
- In round two, nobody got richer. There was just more money chasing the same prize, so the price went up. That is inflation.
- When more `[local currency]` is created, each note in your pocket buys a little less.
- Ask the room: "What did one loaf of bread, or one litre of fuel, cost when you were younger? What does it cost now?" Let a few people answer. [CONFIRM: a local price example the country lead can fill in, e.g. bread or transport fare.]
- This is why saving in a currency that loses value is hard. You work, you save, and the money quietly shrinks.
- Be fair and honest: fiat money is easy to use and convenient. The problem is that a small group can create more of it, and ordinary savers carry the cost.

### 38 to 43, Why this matters, and a bridge (5 min)
Talking points:
- People have long wanted money that no small group can print at will.
- That search is the reason Bitcoin was created, which is our next session.
- Bitcoin has a fixed limit and no one in charge. We will keep it plain and practical.

### 43 to 45, Questions (2 min)
Take one or two questions. Hand out the quiz for the ambassador badge if you are tracking it, or save it for a break.

## Common questions

**"Is all inflation bad?"**
A small, steady rise in prices is normal in most economies. The worry is when money is created fast and savings lose value quickly. Keep it balanced and avoid predictions.

**"If the government controls money, is that illegal to question?"**
No. We are learning how money works, which is ordinary education. We are not giving financial advice or telling anyone to break any law.

**"So should I put all my savings in Bitcoin?"**
This course does not give financial advice, and Bitcoin's price can fall. Today is about understanding, not about telling anyone what to buy.

**"What is a satoshi?"**
One bitcoin can be split into 100 million small units called satoshis, or "sats." Sats are the everyday unit. We cover this properly in Session 3.
