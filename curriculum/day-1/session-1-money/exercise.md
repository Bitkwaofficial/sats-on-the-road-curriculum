# Exercise, Session 1: The inflation auction

**Time:** about 10 minutes
**Group size:** works with 6 to 40 learners
**Goal:** let learners feel, in their own hands, how creating more money pushes prices up and lowers what their money can buy.

## What you need
- Slips of paper to use as play money, all the same.
- Two identical small prizes that people actually want: a sweet, a soda, a piece of fruit. One prize per round.
- A board or flip chart to write the winning prices.

## How to run it

### Round one
1. Give every learner the same amount of play money. For example, 10 slips each.
2. Hold up the first prize. Tell the room it is for sale to the highest bidder, paid in play money.
3. Run a quick, lively auction. Take bids out loud.
4. When the bidding stops, note the winning price on the board under "Round 1."

### Round two
1. Now give every learner a lot more play money. For example, another 40 slips each, so everyone has five times as much.
2. Hold up the second, identical prize.
3. Run the auction again, the same way.
4. Write the new winning price on the board under "Round 2."

### Debrief (keep it short)
Ask the room:
- "Did anyone become richer between round one and round two?"
- "There was only ever one prize each round. So why did the price go up?"

Draw out the answer together: in round two there was more money chasing the same single prize, so the price rose. No new value was created. That is inflation.

Then connect it to real life:
- When more `[local currency]` is created, each note buys a little less.
- Prices rise, and savings quietly lose purchasing power.
- This is why the kind of money you save in matters.

## Facilitator tips
- Do not explain the lesson before round two. The surprise is the teaching.
- Keep both prizes identical so the only thing that changed is the amount of money.
- If the group is large, let learners bid in small teams to keep it quick.
- Stay balanced. Note that some price rises are normal; the point is who gets to create new money and who pays.

## Reflection question for learners
In your own words: if your wages stay the same but prices keep rising, what happens to you over a year? Write one or two sentences.
