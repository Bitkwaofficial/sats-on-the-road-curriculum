# Money, and why ours fails

Session 1, Day 1
Sats On The Road

<!-- notes: Welcome the room. Keep it warm. Ask: what is the most useful thing you own that is not money? Could you pay for lunch with it? -->

---

# What is money?

- A way to trade goods and services
- A shared agreement: it works because everyone accepts it
- Not valuable in itself, it is a tool

<!-- notes: Money is like a common language for trade. Chocolate could be money if everyone accepted it, but it would melt, so it would be bad money. -->

---

# The three jobs of money

- **Store of value:** hold worth to save for later
- **Medium of exchange:** trade it for anything
- **Unit of account:** one way to price and compare

<!-- notes: Without a unit of account you would price a car in cows or in hours of work. Money gives one shared measure. -->

---

# Good money has six properties

- Durable
- Divisible
- Portable
- Acceptable
- Scarce
- Fungible (one unit equals another)

<!-- notes: Pass objects around. Ask which properties each one has. Cowry shells: scarce and portable. Gold: durable and scarce. -->

---

# Step 1: Barter

- Swap goods directly, no money
- Needs a "double coincidence of wants"
- Slow and clumsy

<!-- notes: You have bananas, you want a coconut, but the coconut owner wants a mango. Everyone gets stuck. -->

---

# Step 2: Commodity money

- One good thing everyone accepts
- Cowry shells, gold, silver
- Scarce and hard to fake

<!-- notes: Cowry shells were accepted across parts of Africa for a very long time. These were real money because they were scarce and recognisable. -->

---

# Step 3: Fiat money

- Today's notes and coins
- "Fiat" means "by decree"
- Money because the state says so, not backed by gold

<!-- notes: Over the last century the link between paper money and gold was ended. Now value rests on trust in those who issue it. -->

---

# The inflation auction

- Round 1: everyone gets the same play money
- We auction one small prize
- Round 2: everyone gets much more play money
- We auction the same prize again

<!-- notes: Run the activity now. Do not explain the lesson yet. Write both winning prices on the board. -->

---

# What just happened

- Nobody got richer in round 2
- More money chased the same prize
- The price went up
- That is inflation

<!-- notes: Let them feel it before you name it. The extra money did not create more prizes. -->

---

# Inflation and your [local currency]

- When more [local currency] is created, each note buys less
- Prices rise, savings shrink
- Ask: what did bread or transport cost years ago?

<!-- notes: [CONFIRM: a local price example the country lead fills in]. Keep it honest, no predictions. -->

# Fiat is convenient, but

- Easy to use, cheap to carry
- A small group can create more of it
- Ordinary savers carry the cost

<!-- notes: Be balanced. The problem is not paper itself, it is who can make more of it and who pays. -->

---

# The search for better money

- People long wanted money no small group can print
- That search led to Bitcoin
- Fixed limit, no one in charge
