# Facilitator Guide, Session 3: Hands-on wallet setup, seed phrase, first sats

**Duration:** 90 minutes (the core of Day 1)
**Audience:** Day 1, everyone. This is the hands-on heart of the day. Everyone leaves with a working wallet.

## Goal

By the end, every learner has installed a self-custodial wallet on their own phone, written their seed phrase on paper, verified the backup, received their first sats, and sent sats back to a partner.

## Materials

- Each learner needs a phone. For smartphones, the class wallet. For feature phones, a USSD option like Machankura may be used. [OWNER TO FILL: which options work in this country.]
- A reliable internet connection or data for the room.
- One pen and one sheet or card per learner for the seed phrase. Plain paper is fine.
- A small float of sats to send to each learner for their first receive. [OWNER TO FILL: funding source and amount per learner.]
- Board or flip chart.
- Printed handouts and the checklist in `exercise.md`.
- Two or three helpers who can move around the room. Some learners will be slower, and that is fine.

## Timing (minute by minute)

- **0 to 10, What a wallet is and choosing a self-custodial wallet.** The wallet holds your keys, not the coins. Explain custodial versus self-custodial and why we teach self-custody.
- **10 to 25, Install the wallet, including on an old Android.** Everyone installs the class wallet together, step by step.
- **25 to 45, Write the seed phrase on paper, never a photo.** The slowest and most important part of the day.
- **45 to 55, Verify the backup.** Confirm the words in the app so learners know the paper is correct.
- **55 to 70, Receive the first sats.** Send each learner a small amount and watch it land.
- **70 to 85, Send them back.** Learners pair up and send sats to each other.
- **85 to 90, Wrap, and a note on feature phones (Machankura).** Recap the three rules and point to Session 4.

## Steps

### Choosing a self-custodial wallet (0 to 10)
- A Bitcoin wallet does not hold coins inside it. The coins live on the network. The wallet holds your **keys**, which prove the coins are yours. Repeat: "Not your keys, not your coins."
- Two parts to know:
  - A **receive address**, like your email address. Share it so people can send you sats.
  - A **seed phrase and private key**, like the password to your email. Never share it. Whoever has it controls the money.
- **Custodial** means a company holds your keys. Easy to start, but they can freeze your money. Fine for small amounts and a first taste.
- **Self-custodial** means you hold your own keys. No one can freeze or confiscate your sats, and no one approves your payments. You carry full responsibility, so the seed phrase matters.
- Tell the room which self-custodial wallet you will all use today, and why. Teach one wallet so everyone follows the same screens. [OWNER TO FILL: the class wallet, its publisher, and that it is available here.]
- What to look for in a wallet, in your own words: do you hold your own keys (self-custody), is the code open source, is it simple to use, and does it have a good reputation. Never download a wallet from a link someone sends. Use the official app store and check the maker's name.

### Install the wallet, including on an old Android (10 to 25)
Walk the room through it together. Keep pace so no one is left behind.

1. Open the Google Play Store (Android) or the App Store (iPhone).
2. Search for the class wallet and check the publisher name carefully.
3. Install the app. On an old Android, this is the common snag, so plan for it:
   - If the phone says there is not enough space, help the learner clear a few unused apps or photos, then try again.
   - A Lightning wallet is small and runs well on older Android phones. If one wallet will not install, have a lighter backup wallet ready. [OWNER TO FILL: backup wallet for low-end Android.]
   - If the Play Store itself is out of date and refuses, let it update first, then retry.
4. Open the app and choose "Create a new wallet" (wording varies).
5. Let the app create your keys. Your first receive address is made for you automatically.
6. If the app offers a passcode or PIN, set one. This protects the app on the phone.

For feature-phone learners using USSD, follow the provider's dial-in steps instead. [OWNER TO FILL: the exact USSD steps for your country.]

### Write the seed phrase on paper, never a photo (25 to 45)
Slow right down. This is the single most important skill of the day. Say these clearly and more than once:

- The app shows a list of words, usually 12 or 24. This is your **seed phrase**, also called a recovery phrase. It is the master key to all your sats.
- **Write them on paper, by hand, in the exact order shown.** Number each word.
- **Never take a photo of them.** Photos can sync to the cloud and be stolen.
- **Never type them into any website.** No real wallet or support person will ever ask for your seed phrase. Anyone who asks is trying to rob you.
- **Never store them in notes, messages, or email.**
- Keep the paper somewhere safe and dry that only you can reach. For larger savings, people keep a second copy in a separate safe place.
- If you lose both your phone and your seed phrase, your sats are gone for good. No one can recover them.

Walk the room and check that everyone has actually written the words down before moving on.

### Verify the backup (45 to 55)
- Have each learner confirm the words in the app when it asks. The app checks you wrote them in the right order.
- If the check fails, the learner recopies from the app and tries again. This is why we verify: better to catch a wrong word now than after the phone is lost.
- When the app confirms the backup, have learners fold the paper and keep it on their person for now.

### Receive the first sats (55 to 70)
- To receive, you share your address or a QR code. In the app, tap "Receive." It shows a QR code and an address.
- Explain: your address is safe to share. It is like your email address. Sharing it does not give anyone control of your money.
- Send each learner a small amount of sats to their displayed code. [OWNER TO FILL: amount per learner and funding source.]
- Have them watch the balance update. A ⚡ Lightning payment usually arrives in seconds. Celebrate the first sats calmly and warmly. We cover Lightning properly in Session 4.

### Send them back (70 to 85)
Pair learners up so they can send to each other.

1. Tap "Send."
2. Scan the partner's QR code, or paste their address or invoice.
3. Enter the amount in sats.
4. **Double-check the amount and the recipient before confirming.** Payments cannot be reversed.
5. Confirm and send. Watch it arrive on the other phone.
- Have each pair send a small amount back and forth once or twice, so everyone has both received and sent.

### Wrap, and a note on feature phones (85 to 90)
Recap the three things that matter most:
1. You hold your own keys. Not your keys, not your coins.
2. Your seed phrase is written on paper, never photographed, never typed into a website.
3. You can receive and send sats, and you always check before you confirm.

A note on feature phones: a learner with a basic phone and no smartphone can still use Lightning over USSD with Machankura. There is no app to install and no seed phrase on a screen; the provider secures the account, so treat it like a custodial option and keep only spending money on it. ## Common questions

**"I lost my phone. Are my sats gone?"**
If you have your seed phrase written down, you can restore your wallet on a new phone and your sats are safe. That is exactly why the seed phrase matters.

**"Someone online says they are support and need my seed phrase to help me. Should I give it?"**
Never. No real support ever needs your seed phrase. Anyone who asks for it is trying to steal from you.

**"Can I just take a quick photo of the words, to be safe?"**
No. Photos can sync to the cloud and be stolen. Paper only, by hand.

**"My old Android will not install the app. What now?"**
Clear some space, update the Play Store, and try again. If it still will not install, use the lighter backup wallet, or the USSD option on a feature phone.

**"What if I only have a basic phone?"**
Use Lightning over USSD with Machankura. No smartphone needed. [OWNER TO FILL availability in this country.]

**"Is it safe to share my receive address?"**
Yes. Your address is like your email address. Sharing it lets people pay you. It does not give them any control of your money.

**"How much should I keep in a phone wallet?"**
Keep everyday spending money on your phone. Keep larger savings in a more secure setup, which Day 2 covers for ambassadors.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Rushing the seed phrase step. This is the one part you cannot redo later. Give it the full time.
- Letting learners photograph or type the seed phrase "just this once." Stop it every time.
- Teaching several different wallets at once. Pick one for the room so everyone sees the same screens.
- Skipping the backup check. An unverified seed phrase is a false sense of safety.
- Sending large amounts in the practice. Keep the float tiny; the point is the skill, not the value.
- Forgetting the slow phones. Seat them near a helper and start their install first.
- Promising a wallet or option you have not confirmed works in this country. Check first.
