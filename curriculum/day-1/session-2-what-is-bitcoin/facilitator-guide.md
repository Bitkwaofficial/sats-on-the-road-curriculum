# Facilitator Guide, Session 2: What Bitcoin is, in plain words

**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Audience:** Day 1, everyone. No technical background needed. Builds on Session 1.
**Objectives:**
1. Learners can explain the problem Bitcoin was made to solve.
2. Learners can describe Bitcoin as money that is peer-to-peer, with no one in charge.
3. Learners can explain the fixed limit of 21 million bitcoins and why it matters.
4. Learners can say why Bitcoin is called sound digital money, in plain language.

**Materials:**
- Board or flip chart, markers or chalk.
- Printed handouts, one per learner.
- Slides (optional, `slides.md`).
- For the exercise: enough space for small groups to talk; no phones or apps needed yet.

**Diploma source:** BitKwa Bitcoin Diploma, Chapter 3 (Bitcoin: introduction and how to use it), the introduction section.

---

## Minute by minute

### 0 to 5, Recap and open (5 min)
Recap Session 1 in two lines: money is a shared tool, and fiat money loses value because a small group can create more of it. Then ask:
"If you could design money that no small group could print more of, what would you want it to do?"
Take a couple of answers and keep them on the board.

### 5 to 13, The problem Bitcoin solves (8 min)
Talking points:
- For a long time, people wanted digital money that works like cash, person to person, with no bank in the middle.
- Earlier attempts needed a company or a central office to run them. If that office was shut down or chose to block you, the money stopped working.
- In 2008 a person or group using the name Satoshi Nakamoto shared a short paper describing a different design. In January 2009 the network started running.
- The goal was money that works without trusting a single company or government, and that no one can quietly print more of.

### 13 to 22, What Bitcoin is (9 min)
Keep this plain. Avoid technical words where you can.

Talking points:
- **Bitcoin is money.** It is digital, and it lives on computers all over the world, not in one building.
- **Peer-to-peer:** you can send it straight to another person, the way you hand over cash, without a bank approving it.
- **No one is in charge.** There is no boss, no head office, no single person who can switch it off or change the rules for their own benefit.
- A good picture: Bitcoin is an agreement among many people online. Like a board game, everyone follows the same rules. If someone tries to cheat, the others simply reject them and the game goes on.
- Because the record is copied on so many computers around the world, it is very hard to stop or fake.

### 22 to 30, The 21 million limit and sound digital money (8 min)
Talking points:
- One rule is built in and cannot be changed by anyone: there will only ever be **21 million** bitcoins.
- Nobody can create extra for themselves. If they tried, every other computer running the software would reject it.
- Compare this to Session 1: fiat money can be created at will, which lowers its value. Bitcoin has a fixed limit, so no small group can dilute it.
- One bitcoin splits into 100 million small units called **satoshis**, or "sats." Sats are the everyday unit. You do not need a whole bitcoin to use it.
- This is why people call Bitcoin **sound digital money**: it is scarce, it is hard to counterfeit, and it is open for anyone with a phone and internet to use.
- Be honest and balanced: Bitcoin is still young, like the internet in the 1990s. Its price measured in `[local currency]` can rise and fall. It is a tool to learn and use, not a promise of profit.

### 30 to 38, Exercise: rules of the game (8 min)
Run the activity in `exercise.md`. Small groups act out a simple game with a shared rule, show why a cheater is rejected, and see why no one can simply invent extra tokens.

### 38 to 43, Why it matters, honest caveats (5 min)
Talking points:
- Bitcoin gives ordinary people a way to hold and move money that does not depend on one company's permission.
- With that freedom comes responsibility, which we practice in the next session when each of you sets up a wallet.
- Repeat the honest notes: no financial advice, no price predictions, the price can fall.

### 43 to 45, Questions (2 min)
Take one or two questions. Point to the glossary for any new words.

## Common questions

**"Who prints Bitcoin, then?"**
No one can print it freely. New bitcoins are released slowly by the network itself on a fixed schedule, and the total can never pass 21 million.

**"Is Bitcoin a company I can call if something goes wrong?"**
No. There is no head office and no hotline. That is its strength and its responsibility. We cover how to stay safe in Session 3.

**"Is it only for rich or technical people?"**
No. Anyone with a basic phone and internet can use it. There are even ways to use it on a feature phone without a smartphone, which we mention in Session 3.

**"Is it real money if I cannot hold it?"**
Most money today is already digital, sitting in bank computers. Bitcoin is digital too, but no single company controls it.

**"Will I get rich?"**
This course gives no financial advice and makes no predictions. Treat Bitcoin as money to understand and use, not a bet.
