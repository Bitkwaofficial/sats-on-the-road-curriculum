# Exercise, Session 2: Rules of the game

**Time:** about 8 minutes
**Group size:** split the room into small groups of 4 to 6
**Goal:** feel, through a quick role play, why Bitcoin works as an agreement among people, why a cheater gets rejected, and why no one can simply invent extra money.

No phones or apps are needed for this exercise. It is all done with people and paper.

## Setup
- Give each group 10 identical paper tokens to share. These stand in for a fixed money supply.
- Tell each group one rule: "Only these paper tokens count as money. There are 10, and that is all there will ever be."

## Part 1: Trade by the rules (about 3 min)
1. Ask each group to do a few quick pretend trades among themselves using the tokens. For example, one learner "sells" a pretend mango for 2 tokens.
2. Let them pass tokens back and forth a few times.
3. Point out: trade is easy when everyone agrees on the same money and the same rule.

## Part 2: The cheater (about 2 min)
1. Quietly ask one person in each group to try to pay with something that is not a token, for example a torn scrap of paper or a leaf.
2. Ask the group: "Do you accept it?"
3. They will say no. Draw out the lesson: the group agrees on the rules, so a cheater is simply rejected and the trading continues. No central referee was needed. The group itself enforced the rule.

## Part 3: Printing extra (about 2 min)
1. Now ask one person in each group to try to add 5 brand new tokens they made themselves.
2. Ask the group: "Are these real? Do they count?"
3. The group should refuse them, because everyone agreed there are only 10. This is the fixed-supply rule of Bitcoin: no one can create extra for themselves, because everyone else rejects it.

## Debrief (about 1 min)
Ask the whole room:
- "Who was in charge of each group's money?" (No single person. The group agreed together.)
- "Why could no one cheat or print more?" (Because the others checked and rejected it.)

Connect it to Bitcoin: thousands of computers around the world play this same role, all the time. They all follow the same rules, so no one can cheat and no one can make extra beyond the 21 million limit.

## Reflection question for learners
In one sentence: why is it hard to cheat a system where everyone checks the rules together?
