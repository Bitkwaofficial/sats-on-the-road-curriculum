# Session 7, Bitcoin for activists and journalists: privacy and opsec

**Duration:** 90 minutes
**Audience:** Day 2, future ambassadors
**Diploma source:** Chapter 6 (Bitcoin for Activism: Privacy and Human Rights)
**Companion docs:** [opsec handout](../../../safety/opsec-for-activists-and-journalists.md) ·
[team safety protocol](../../../safety/team-safety-protocol.md)

## Objectives

By the end, ambassadors can:

1. Explain why financial privacy is a safety issue, not a way to hide wrongdoing.
2. Explain that Bitcoin is pseudonymous, not anonymous, and what follows from that.
3. Teach the seven opsec habits and the scam rules to someone at risk.
4. Help an activist, journalist or group receive support safely (separate wallets,
   Lightning, no address reuse, multisig for group funds).
5. Know what to do, and not do, with authorities and seized devices.

## Materials

- Printed [opsec handout](../../../safety/opsec-for-activists-and-journalists.md),
  one per person (translated).
- Two phones with wallets for the live demonstration.
- Slides ([`slides.md`](./slides.md)).

## Minute-by-minute

**0-10, Why this matters (story first).**
Open with a real case from the handout: Nigeria #EndSARS (2020), where organisers'
bank accounts were frozen and the Feminist Coalition kept the movement funded with
Bitcoin; or Kenya 2024, where fundraisers were delayed and mobile-money transfers
blocked. Ask: "Has anyone here had money held, delayed or questioned?" Let a few
people answer. Land the point: money can be switched off, so it matters to hold
value that cannot be.

**10-20, Money, power and rights.**
Walk through how financial control becomes political control: account freezes,
de-banking under vague rules, transaction censorship, surveillance. Keep it
concrete and local. `[OWNER TO FILL: a local example if one exists]`

**20-35, The one key idea: pseudonymous, not anonymous.**
Explain the public blockchain: every on-chain payment is permanent and visible;
your name is not on it, but patterns and cash-out points can reveal you. Contrast
anonymity (no one knows you) with pseudonymity (a traceable identity without your
name). Conclusion: privacy must be practised, not assumed.

**35-60, The seven habits (teach them to teach it).**
Go through the seven habits from the handout, with a live demonstration on two
phones:
1. Never reuse an address (show a wallet making a fresh address each time).
2. Never share a screenshot, balance or xpub.
3. Separate wallets by purpose (donations / spending / savings).
4. Prefer Lightning for small, frequent payments (more private, cheaper).
5. Protect the device (lock, PIN, updates, clean phone for sensitive work).
6. Pseudonyms and a Lightning address for public fundraising; no live balances.
7. Sane custody: paper/metal seed, two locations, multisig for group funds.

**60-75, Scams, phishing and custody mistakes.**
The golden rule, say it twice: **if anyone asks for your seed phrase or private
key, it is a scam.** Cover fake wallet links, "restore your wallet" messages,
"double your Bitcoin", and poor custody (cloud backups, screenshots, one phone
holding a group's whole fund).

**75-88, Group funds and seized devices.**
Why one person should not hold a group's keys. Introduce multisig in plain words:
several people must agree to move money, so one arrest or one seized phone cannot
drain the fund. Walk through the [team safety protocol](../../../safety/team-safety-protocol.md)
steps for a seized device or a detention. Stress: plan this before you need it.

**88-90, Close and the law.**
Repeat the boundary clearly: none of this is about evading legitimate law or hiding
a crime. It is about ordinary people keeping the ability to organise, report and
survive. Hand out the [exercise](./exercise.md) and the
[quiz](./quiz.md).

## Common questions

- **"Is this legal?"** Using Bitcoin is legal in most places, but rules differ and
  change. Teach the law where you are; never advise anyone to break it.
- **"Can the government see my Bitcoin?"** They can see the public chain. They
  usually learn who you are at the edges: KYC exchanges, reused addresses, leaks.
  The habits reduce that exposure.
- **"What about mixing tools I read about?"** Some have been shut down or made
  legally risky, and the picture changes fast. Teach the durable habits; check any
  specific tool's status and legality first.
- **"Isn't wanting privacy suspicious?"** No. Privacy is dignity. You lock your
  door without hiding a crime.
