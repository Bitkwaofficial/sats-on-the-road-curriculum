# Exercise, Coach someone at risk

Work in pairs. One person is the **ambassador**, the other is a **journalist or
activist** who needs to receive support safely. Then swap roles.

## Scenario

The journalist has been covering a sensitive story. A supporter abroad wants to
send them money, but their bank account was recently questioned. They have a basic
smartphone. Help them set up to receive support safely.

## The ambassador's task (about 10 minutes per turn)

Walk the journalist through, in plain words:

1. **Set up a separate receiving wallet**, not the one they use for daily spending.
2. **Get a Lightning address** (or show how the wallet makes a fresh address each
   time) so they do not publish one fixed on-chain address.
3. **Agree what is safe to post publicly:** a pseudonym or collective name, never a
   live balance, never a screenshot.
4. **Protect the phone:** screen lock, strong PIN, updates, and the idea of a clean
   device for sensitive work.
5. **State the golden rule:** no one, ever, needs their seed phrase.
6. **Plan for a bad day:** where the seed backup lives, and who to call if the
   phone is lost or taken (point to the team safety protocol).

## The journalist's task

Play it honestly. Ask the hard questions a real person asks:
"Can the government see this?", "Is this legal?", "What if I lose my phone?",
"Someone offered to double my Bitcoin, should I?"

## Debrief (5 minutes, whole room)

- What was the hardest thing to explain simply?
- Did anyone almost skip the device-security part? (It is the most forgotten.)
- Which answer about the law did you give, and was it accurate for here?

## Self-check

You did well if the journalist left with a separate receiving wallet, a safe way
to be paid, a plan for their seed phrase, and the golden rule memorised, without
you ever touching their seed phrase or their phone yourself.
