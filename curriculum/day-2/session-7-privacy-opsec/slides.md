# Bitcoin for activists and journalists
### Privacy and opsec
Day 2 · Session 7 · 90 minutes

<!-- notes: Open with a story, not theory. Ask who has had money held or questioned. -->

---

## Money can be switched off

- Nigeria, 2020: organisers' bank accounts frozen; Bitcoin kept the movement funded
- Kenya, 2024: fundraisers delayed, mobile-money transfers blocked
- Zimbabwe, Sudan: inflation, blocked remittances, audits used against civil society

<!-- notes: Keep it concrete and local. The point: hold value that cannot be turned off. -->

---

## Money, power and rights

- Control money, and you control movement, speech and assembly
- Tactics: account freezes, de-banking, transaction censorship, surveillance
- Financial privacy is a **safety** issue, not a way to hide wrongdoing

---

## The one idea to remember

**Bitcoin is pseudonymous, not anonymous.**

- Every on-chain payment is public and permanent
- Your name is not on it, but patterns can reveal you
- So privacy must be *practised*, not assumed

<!-- notes: Contrast anonymity (no one knows you) vs pseudonymity (traceable, no name). -->

---

## Seven habits (1-4)

1. Never reuse an address
2. Never share a screenshot, balance or xpub
3. Separate wallets: donations / spending / savings
4. Prefer Lightning for small, frequent payments

<!-- notes: Demonstrate a fresh address on a real wallet here. -->

---

## Seven habits (5-7)

5. Protect the device: lock, PIN, updates, clean phone for sensitive work
6. Pseudonyms and a Lightning address for public fundraising; no live balances
7. Sane custody: paper or metal seed, two locations, multisig for group funds

---

## The golden rule

# If anyone asks for your seed phrase, it is a scam.

- Fake wallet links on WhatsApp or Telegram
- "Your wallet is locked, click to restore"
- "Double your Bitcoin", guaranteed returns

---

## Group funds and seized devices

- One person should not hold a group's keys
- **Multisig:** several people must agree to move money
- One arrest or one seized phone cannot drain the fund
- Plan the response *before* you need it (see team safety protocol)

---

## The boundary

- This is **not** about evading legitimate law or hiding a crime
- It is about ordinary people keeping the ability to organise, report and survive
- Teach the law where you are; never advise breaking it

---

## Hand out and practise

- Opsec handout (one page, translated)
- Exercise: coach a journalist through safe receiving
- Short quiz for the ambassador badge
