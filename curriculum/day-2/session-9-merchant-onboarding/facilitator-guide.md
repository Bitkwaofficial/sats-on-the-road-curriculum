# Session 9, Onboarding a merchant, step by step

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Audience** | Day 2, future ambassadors (people who will teach others and onboard merchants) |
| **Source** | New material for Sats On The Road. See the [merchant guide](../../../merchant-kit/merchant-guide.md) and the [merchant onboarding checklist](../../../merchant-kit/merchant-onboarding-checklist.md) |

## Objectives

By the end of this session, each ambassador can:

1. Explain the value of accepting Bitcoin to a busy merchant in under a minute.
2. Set up a merchant to receive payments with a Lightning address or wallet.
3. Run a test payment and show the merchant how to confirm it arrived.
4. Show the merchant their cash-out options honestly.
5. Leave the stall set up and signed, and know how to follow up.

## Materials

- Two phones per pair: one as the "merchant", one as the "customer".
- The teaching wallet installed on both, `[primary wallet, e.g. Blink or Phoenix]`.
- Printed [merchant onboarding checklist](../../../merchant-kit/merchant-onboarding-checklist.md), one per person.
- Printed "Bitcoin accepted here" signs and QR stands from the [merchant kit](../../../merchant-kit).
- A small amount of sats on the customer phone to make real test payments.
- Projector for `slides.md`.

## Before you start

Onboarding a merchant is how a town's Bitcoin economy actually starts. A learner who can spend sats needs a merchant who can receive them. The ambassador's job is to make the merchant feel safe, set them up in minutes, and leave them able to do it alone. Respect the merchant's time above all; they are working while you talk.

---

## Minute-by-minute plan

### 0–5 min, Why merchants matter (5)
No merchants, no circular economy. Every onboarded merchant gives dozens of learners a real place to spend. Set the tone: we meet merchants where they are, we are quick, and we never pressure.

### 5–13 min, The value, in under a minute (8)
Teach the short pitch a merchant will actually listen to:
- Get paid directly, with no chargebacks once confirmed.
- Low fees on Lightning compared with many card or transfer options.
- Reach customers who hold Bitcoin, including visitors and the road trip crowd.
- Cash out to `[local currency]` when they want, using options we will show them.
Keep it honest: the price of Bitcoin can move, which is why we also show same-day cash-out. Practise saying it in one breath.

### 13–25 min, Set up receiving (12)
Walk the setup:
- Install the wallet from the official source only, or use the merchant's existing one.
- Help them create and back up a seed phrase (apply Session 6 rules; never handle it for them).
- Find the Lightning address or the "Receive" screen and QR code.
- Decide how they will show the QR: on the phone, or on a printed stand at the counter.
Many small merchants will start with an easy Lightning wallet; be clear about custodial vs self-custody so they can choose.

### 25–32 min, Do a test payment (7)
Make a small real payment from the customer phone to the merchant. Let the merchant watch their own screen. The moment they see it land is the moment they believe. Then show them how to read the amount in `[local currency]`.

### 32–40 min, Confirm it arrived and cash out (8)
Show the merchant where confirmed payments appear in the wallet history, so they can check any sale later without you. Then walk cash-out honestly: options may include `[CONFIRM per country: e.g. Vexl P2P, Banxaas, BitSpenda, Machankura]`. Explain each only where it actually works locally, and never promise a rate.

### 40–45 min, Leave the signage and follow up (5)
Place the "Bitcoin accepted here" sign and the QR stand where customers will see them. Add the merchant to the stop's record. Agree a simple follow-up: a message or a return visit to answer questions and check the first customer sales.

### 45–58 min, Role-play onboarding (13)
Run the exercise (see `exercise.md`). In pairs, one plays a busy merchant, one plays the ambassador, working straight down the checklist from pitch to signage. Then swap.

### 58–60 min, Wrap (2)
One takeaway each. Point to the quiz for the badge and to the merchant kit for the printable materials.

**Total: 60 minutes.**

## Talking points

- Sell the problem you solve, not the technology. "Get paid directly, cash out when you like" beats any explanation of blockchains.
- Respect their time. If they are serving a customer, wait or come back. A rushed merchant is a lost merchant.
- The test payment does the convincing. Talk less, show the sats landing.
- Never touch their seed phrase. Guide; let them write and keep it.
- Honesty about price and cash-out builds the trust that keeps the sign on the wall after you leave.

## Common questions

**"Will I lose money if the price drops?"**
You can cash out to `[local currency]` the same day if you prefer, so you hold Bitcoin only as long as you choose. Never promise a price direction.

**"Is this legal for my business?"**
`[CONFIRM: legal and tax status per country]`. Give the honest local answer and point to the country lead if unsure.

**"What are the fees?"**
Lightning fees are usually very small. Show the real fee on a test payment rather than quoting a number.

**"What if a customer says they paid but I did not get it?"**
Check the wallet history together. A confirmed payment shows there. If it is not confirmed, it did not arrive, so wait or ask them to try again.
