# Session 6, Self-custody deeper: backups, recovery, what not to do

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Audience** | Day 2, future ambassadors (people who will teach others and onboard merchants) |
| **Source** | BitKwa Bitcoin Diploma, Chapter 3 (self-custody) and Chapter 5 (how Bitcoin works) |

## Objectives

By the end of this session, each ambassador can:

1. Explain in plain words why "not your keys, not your coins" matters, and the honest trade-off between custodial and self-custodial wallets.
2. Back up a seed phrase using at least two safe methods, and say out loud the methods that are never safe.
3. Test a recovery: restore a wallet from a seed phrase and confirm the balance returns.
4. Name the common ways people lose their Bitcoin (screenshots, cloud, sharing, fake-wallet scams) and how to avoid each.
5. Decide when a hardware or cold-storage wallet is worth it for savings.

## Materials

- One or two smartphones per pair, with the teaching wallet installed, `[primary wallet, e.g. Blink or Phoenix]`.
- A spare "practice" wallet already holding a tiny amount of sats for the recovery test.
- Printed seed-phrase backup cards (12 or 24 blank numbered lines), two per person.
- Pens that do not smudge. Optional: a sample metal backup plate to pass around.
- The handout for this session (the "How Bitcoin works" one-pager) printed for each person.
- Projector for `slides.md`.

## Before you start

This is the ambassador day. These people will soon be the only Bitcoin help in their town. They must be able to protect their own coins first, and then teach backup and recovery calmly to a nervous beginner. Keep the tone steady, not scary. Self-custody is responsibility, not danger.

## Minute-by-minute plan

### 0–5 min, Why this session (5)
Recap Day 1: everyone set up a wallet and wrote down a seed phrase. Today we go deeper, because an ambassador has to keep coins safe for years, not just for a demo. Ask: "Who already feels unsure about where their seed phrase is right now?" Normalise the honest answer.

### 5–13 min, Custodial vs self-custody, honestly (8)
Walk the custodial vs self-custodial trade-off (slide). Custodial is easy and has a reset button, but the provider holds the keys, can freeze the account, and can be hacked or collapse. Self-custody means you hold the keys: no freeze, no confiscation, but no reset button either. Land the saying: "not your keys, not your coins." Be honest: a brand-new user may start custodial, but an ambassador should move to self-custody and know how to do it.

### 13–25 min, Backing up a seed phrase (12)
The seed phrase (12 or 24 words) IS the wallet. Anyone with the words controls the coins; losing the words with no backup loses the coins forever. Demonstrate safe backups:
- Write it on paper or card, in order, and store it somewhere private and dry.
- Keep a second copy in a different safe place, so one fire or flood does not end it.
- For larger savings, a metal backup survives water and fire.
Then say clearly what is NEVER safe: a screenshot or photo, a note in the cloud or email, a message to yourself on chat, or reading the words aloud to anyone. Have each person write a practice phrase on a card by hand.

### 25–38 min, Test a recovery (13)
A backup you have never tested is a guess. Run the exercise (see `exercise.md`): in pairs, restore the practice wallet from its seed phrase on a second phone and confirm the sats appear. The point they must feel: the coins live on the network, not in the phone. The phone can break and the coins come back, as long as the words are safe.

### 38–50 min, Common mistakes and scams (12)
Go through how people actually lose coins:
- Screenshots and cloud backups that get hacked or synced out.
- Sharing the seed phrase with anyone, including "support staff". No real support ever needs it.
- Typing the seed phrase into a website or a pop-up.
- Fake-wallet apps that look real and steal the phrase on setup. Install only from the official source and verify the developer.
- "Send 1, get 2 back", giveaways, and "the next Bitcoin". Apply "don't trust, verify".
Ask ambassadors to add scams they have seen locally.

### 50–58 min, Cold storage for savings (8)
Separate spending money from savings. A mobile wallet is fine for daily spending. For savings you want to keep for a long time, a hardware (cold) wallet keeps the keys offline, away from malware. Explain the trade-off: more secure, costs money, and the same rule applies, back up the seed and test the recovery. `[OWNER TO FILL: whether a specific hardware wallet is recommended per country]`.

### 58–60 min, Wrap (2)
One sentence each: "My seed phrase is safe when ___." Point to the quiz for the ambassador badge, and to Session 7 (privacy and opsec) coming next.

## Talking points

- The coins are not "in" the phone. They are entries on a shared ledger. The seed phrase is the only thing that proves they are yours.
- Self-custody is like keeping money at home: no one can freeze it or take it, and no one will rescue you if you lose the key. That is the deal, both halves.
- A backup you have not tested is not a backup. Testing recovery once is worth more than ten careful warnings.
- Convenience is the enemy of key safety. Every shortcut (photo, cloud, "I will remember it") is how coins get lost or stolen.
- As an ambassador, never ask a learner to show you their seed phrase, and teach them that no honest person ever will.

## Common questions

**"What if I lose my phone?"**
If the seed phrase is backed up, buy or borrow another phone, install the wallet, restore from the phrase, and the coins return. The phone is just a window.

**"What if someone sees my seed phrase?"**
Treat it as if the coins are gone. Move the coins to a new wallet with a new phrase straight away, and keep the new phrase private.

**"Is a custodial wallet always bad?"**
No. It is a reasonable first step for a beginner holding a small amount. The honest message is: small and short-term can be custodial, savings should be self-custody.

**"Can I just remember the words in my head?"**
Not safely. Memory fails, and if something happens to you, your family cannot recover the coins. Write it down and store it well.

**"Do I need a hardware wallet?"**
Only if you are saving a meaningful amount for the long term. For small daily spending, a well-backed-up mobile wallet is enough.

**"Someone offered to double my sats if I send first. Real?"**
No. That is always a scam. Don't trust, verify.
