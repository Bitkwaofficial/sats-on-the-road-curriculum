# How to teach this, a one-page reminder

Keep this with you when you run a session. You already know enough. Teach to the most unsure person in the room, and let people do things with their own hands.

## The shape of a session

| Part | Time (2-hour class) | What happens |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| Open | 10 min | Welcome, why money matters here, what they will leave with |
| Teach and do | 85 min | Money and why ours fails, what Bitcoin is, set up a wallet, one real Lightning payment |
| Close | 15 min | Questions, where to get help, how to keep going |
| Buffer | 10 min | It always runs long |

## Five things that make a good facilitator

1. **Do, then explain.** Get phones in hands early. People remember what they did.
2. **Speak plainly.** No jargon. Use local prices, names, and examples.
3. **Respect the room.** Many have run households and businesses for years. Teach with them, not at them.
4. **Keep time.** Start on time. Cut depth, never the hands-on part. Protect the real payment at the end.
5. **Be honest.** "I don't know, let's find out" beats a confident wrong answer every time.

## Keeping people engaged

- Ask a question, then stay quiet and wait for the answer.
- Pair people up so the shy ones are helped by a neighbour.
- Move around the room and use names.
- Celebrate the first successful payment out loud. It lifts the whole room.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Talking too long.
- Using words beginners do not know.
- Skipping the part where people do it themselves.
- Pretending to know an answer.
- Running out of time before anyone makes a real payment.

## When you are stuck

Write the question down. Bring the answer to the next session, or ask your country lead. Honesty grows trust.

---

*A session succeeds when people leave having done something, not having heard something.*
