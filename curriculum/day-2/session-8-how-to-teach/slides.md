# How to teach this

From learner to facilitator

Session 8 · Day 2 · 60 min

<!-- notes: Two days ago these people were beginners. That is their advantage, not a weakness. -->

---

# You are ready sooner than you think

You do not need to be an expert.

You need to be one step ahead, and honest about the rest.

<!-- notes: The main message. Say it plainly and mean it. -->

---

# How adults learn

- By doing first, understanding second
- When it solves a real money problem they have
- When they are respected, not talked down to
- They remember what they did, not what they heard

<!-- notes: Hands on the phone early. Connect every idea to money they use. -->

---

# Say it, show it, let them do it

One idea, three ways.

Repetition in different forms beats saying it louder.

<!-- notes: People remember little from a lecture and a lot from one thing they did. -->

---

# Shape of a two-hour session

- **Open (10 min):** welcome, why money matters, what they leave with
- **Teach and do (85 min):** money, what Bitcoin is, set up a wallet, one real payment
- **Close (15 min):** questions, where to get help, how to keep going
- **Buffer (10 min):** it always runs long

<!-- notes: This is the one-session plan from Day 1. Keep the real payment sacred. -->

---

# Managing time

- Start on time, even with a small crowd
- Keep one eye on the clock
- Cut depth, not the hands-on part
- Always protect the real payment at the end

<!-- notes: Running long is the number one new-facilitator problem. -->

---

# Keep the room awake

- Ask questions, then wait for answers
- Use local prices, local names, local examples
- Get phones in hands early and often
- Pair people so neighbours help each other

<!-- notes: Move around. Use names. Celebrate the first payment out loud. -->

---

# Practice-teach

Five minutes each, in pairs.

Partner plays an unsure beginner.

Then swap, and give one kind, specific note.

<!-- notes: This is the exercise and the heart of the session. Circulate and collect good moments. -->

---

# Mistakes to avoid

- Talking too long
- Using jargon
- Skipping the hands-on part
- Pretending to know an answer
- Running out of time before the real payment

<!-- notes: Use the debrief to surface these gently from the pairs' own experience. -->

---

# When you do not know

"I don't know. Let's find out together."

Honesty builds more trust than a confident wrong answer.

<!-- notes: Write the question down and bring the answer next time. -->

---

# A session succeeds when

People leave having **done** something,

not having **heard** something.

<!-- notes: Close here. This is the measure of good teaching. -->
