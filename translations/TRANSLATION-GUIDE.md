# Translation Guide

Thank you for helping translate *Sats On The Road* into your language. A handout
that reads naturally in the local language is worth far more than a perfect one in
English that nobody in the market can follow. This guide explains what to
translate first, how to keep words consistent, and how to send your work back to
us, even from a phone.

## What to translate, in order

Please work in this order. Each piece builds on the one before it.

1. **The glossary first.** Open [`GLOSSARY.md`](./GLOSSARY.md) and agree one word
   per term for your language. Everything else uses these words, so settling them
   first keeps every handout and slide consistent. If a term has no good local
   word yet, keep the English or French word and add a short explanation.
2. **The Day 1 handouts.** These are the one-page sheets learners hold during the
   first day (the wallet setup sheet, the Lightning sheet, the market practice
   sheet). They matter most because the most people read them.
3. **The merchant sign.** The printable "Bitcoin accepted here" sign from the
   merchant kit. Short, but it goes on the wall of every shop, so the wording
   should feel right.
4. **The consent form.** The form people sign before a photo or video is taken of
   them. This protects people, so the translation must be clear and accurate, not
   rushed. If anything is unclear, ask before guessing.

Later pieces (the full slides, the ambassador playbook, the safety material) come
after these four. Do not feel you must do everything. One well-translated handout
is a real contribution.

## How terms stay consistent

- **Use the glossary as the single source of truth.** When a Bitcoin term appears
  in a handout, use exactly the word your language agreed in
  [`GLOSSARY.md`](./GLOSSARY.md). Do not invent a second word for the same term in
  a different file.
- If you find a better word while translating, change it **in the glossary first**,
  then update the handouts that use it. That way the next translator sees your
  improvement.
- Keep bitcoin units in plain terms: 1 bitcoin (₿) is 100,000,000 satoshis
  ("sats"). Sats are the everyday unit. Use the **local currency** in examples,
  for instance `[local currency]`, so prices feel real.
- Leave placeholders in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` as they are. They are slots a country
  lead fills in later (a date, a wallet name, a currency). Translate the words
  around them, not the bracketed slot itself.
- Keep the ⚡ Lightning label and the ₿ bitcoin sign as they are. Do not add
  decorative symbols.

## A few style notes

- Short, warm, plain sentences. Write the way you would explain to a neighbour in
  the market, not the way a bank writes.
- No price predictions and no promises of profit. Bitcoin's price can fall, and
  the handouts say so honestly. Keep that honesty in your language.
- Keep each handout to roughly one page so it still prints on one sheet.

## How to submit a translation from a phone

You do not need a computer. You can do all of this in a phone browser on GitHub.

1. **Open the repository** on github.com and sign in (a free account is enough).
2. **Find your language folder**, for example `translations/fr/` for French, and
   open the file you want to work on. If the file does not exist yet, open your
   language folder's `README.md` to see what is needed.
3. **Tap the pencil icon** (Edit) at the top of the file. GitHub will offer to
   make your own copy (a "fork") so you can edit safely. Tap yes.
4. **Type your translation.** You can paste from your notes app.
5. **Scroll down and tap "Commit changes"** (or "Propose changes"). Write a short
   note like "French: Day 1 wallet handout".
6. **Tap "Create pull request".** That sends your work to us to review and merge.
   We may suggest small changes; that is normal and part of the process.

Prefer not to use GitHub? Open an
["Offer a translation" issue](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/offer-a-translation.md)
instead and we will help you get your work in.
